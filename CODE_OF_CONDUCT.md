# Code of conduct

OpenNote is made by people with different backgrounds, skills, and amounts of experience. This code explains how we treat each other so that everyone can take part and do good work. It covers every OpenNote space: the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, and any chat or event run for the project. It also covers anyone speaking for OpenNote in public.

## Our standard

Everyone is welcome here, whatever their identity, background, or level of skill. Disagreement is normal and useful. Disrespect is not. When in doubt, choose the kinder reading of what someone wrote, and the kinder way to write your reply.

## Expected behavior

- Be patient and helpful, especially with people who are new to the project or to open source.
- Assume good intent. Ask a question before you draw a conclusion.
- Review ideas and code, not people. When you ask for a change, explain the reason.
- Take feedback calmly, and say so when you were wrong.
- Keep threads on topic, and respect the time of the people reading them.
- Give credit for other people's work and ideas.
- Respect privacy. Share personal details or private messages only with the author's permission.
- Look out for each other. Speak up when something seems wrong, or report it.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, or intimidation, in public or in private.
- Insults, slurs, and jokes that put down a person or a group.
- Sexual comments, images, or attention that the other person did not ask for.
- Posting someone's private information, such as a home address or phone number, without their consent.
- Trolling, spam, or deliberately derailing a discussion.
- Contacting someone again after they asked you to stop.
- Encouraging or helping someone else do any of the above.

## How to report

If someone breaks this code, or you feel unsafe, report it privately. Please don't report conduct problems in a public issue or thread. You have two options:

- **GitHub's "Report abuse":** use the "..." menu on the comment or issue and choose "Report content", or use "Block or report" on the person's profile. GitHub's staff review these reports. If GitHub also offers "Report to repository admins", choose it so the maintainers see the report too.
- **A private message to the maintainers:** open a [private security advisory](https://github.com/XrxcGH/OpenNote/security/advisories/new). Only you and the maintainers can see it. Start the title with "Conduct report" so it isn't mistaken for a security issue.

Include what happened, where and when, who was involved, and links or screenshots if you have them. You don't need proof to report. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first.

The maintainers aim to reply within seven days. They keep the reporter's identity private and share details only with the people handling the report. If a report involves a maintainer, that person takes no part in handling it. When no other maintainer can handle it, report it to GitHub as described above. Nobody will face retaliation for a report made in good faith.

## Enforcement

The maintainers decide whether this code was broken and what to do about it. They may edit, hide, or remove comments, commits, issues, and other contributions that break it. They choose a step based on how serious the behavior was and whether it has happened before:

1. **Correction:** a private message that explains what was wrong and asks for a change. A public apology may be requested.
2. **Warning:** a formal warning, which may include a period of no contact with the people involved. Further problems lead to a ban.
3. **Temporary ban:** no participation in any OpenNote space for a set time.
4. **Permanent ban:** no participation in any OpenNote space from then on.

Serious behavior, such as threats or sustained harassment, can go straight to a ban. The maintainers explain each decision to the person involved. That person can appeal once, through the same private channel. Where possible, a maintainer who wasn't involved reviews the appeal.

These steps are inspired by the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), but the text of this code is original to OpenNote.
