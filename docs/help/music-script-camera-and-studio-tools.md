# Music, script, camera, and studio tools

One tool window holds the tools for music, film and theater, and visual art. Each tool has a tab. A tool takes typed values, works the problem, and shows the results, the worked steps, and its assumptions. These tools are new in Beta and off in Stable. Check the answers against your own work while you learn them.

## Open the tools

- Press Ctrl+K and type "music", "script", "camera", or "studio". Each tool also has its own command, such as "voice leading" or "ear training", which opens the window on that tab.
- The window remembers the last tab and what you typed in each tool.
- Insert puts the work into the page: the answer line, the results table, the worked steps, a code block, or a picture.
- Play, where it appears, plays the answer with a built-in synthesizer. Nothing is downloaded and nothing is recorded.

## Music

- **Score block:** write music in ABC notation and see it engraved, hear it, and move it to another key or interval. It also writes the part for a transposing instrument, and shows the tune as numbered notation, as sargam, or as guitar tablature. The bars are added up and checked against the meter.
- **Music theory:** name intervals, find the note above or below a note, and turn notes into MIDI numbers and frequencies for any A4. Spell scales and modes from Western, Hindustani, Carnatic (all 72 melakarta scales by rule), Japanese, and Chinese traditions, and list maqam and gamelan scales in cents, marked as approximate. Spell chords from symbols, name chords from notes, find key signatures and Roman numerals (secondary, borrowed, Neapolitan, and augmented sixth chords), transpose for instruments, work out pitch-class sets with prime forms and Forte names, build twelve-tone matrices, look up jazz scales and ii–V–I progressions, and find guitar chord shapes.
- **Voice-leading checker:** check four-part chorale writing and two-voice species counterpoint against common-practice rules. Each problem names the chord or measure, the voices, and the rule. Pick a relaxed, standard, or strict rule set, because textbooks differ.
- **Ear training:** seeded drills for intervals, chord qualities, scales and modes, melodic and rhythmic dictation, and sight-singing. The same seed gives the same items. A session of answers is graded together, and missed items are given review dates by the same schedule the flashcards use.
- **Tuning and rhythm:** compare tunings and temperaments in cents, work out beat rates and the harmonic series, and practice rhythm with a metronome, Euclidean rhythms, polyrhythms, and the cycles of several traditions.

## Film and theater

- **Script formatter:** write a screenplay or stage play in Fountain, a plain-text format, and see it laid out. It estimates pages (55 lines to a page) and running time (about a minute a page), and lists scenes, characters, and locations. It also seeds breakdown sheets with the cast, props, and notes from the text.
- **Camera, timing, and lighting:** exposure and equivalent settings, depth of field, field of view, shutter angle, frame rates, timecode (including drop-frame), footage, animation planning, light levels, lighting ratios, color temperature, and media storage.

## Art and design

- **Scale, color, and materials:** scale drawings and models, enlarging with a grid, color contrast and color-vision checks, color spaces and differences, palettes, type scales, perspective grids, and quantities of fabric, glaze, and other materials.

## Where the data comes from

Every reference value is public domain, openly licensed, or worked out by a rule written for the app, and the source is named under the result. Maqam and gamelan tunings are teaching approximations. Players and instruments differ.
