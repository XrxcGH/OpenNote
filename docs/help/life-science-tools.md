# Life science tools

Work physiology, pharmacology, neuroscience, lab, population, and ecology problems with units and worked steps, then insert the answer into your page.
These tools are new in Beta and are for coursework only: they never give clinical, dosing, or treatment advice.

## Open the tools

- Open the command palette and choose Open life science tools, or a single tool such as Open physiology calculators.
- The window has a tab for each tool. Use the arrow keys to move between tabs, and the Calculation list to pick a calculation.
- Most answers update as you type. Simulations show a Run button, and each stochastic one has a seed, so the same seed gives the same result.
- Insert puts the answer line, the worked steps, the equation as LaTeX, the result table, or a picture of the plot at the caret.

## Body systems and drugs

- **Physiology calculators**: heart and blood flow, breathing and oxygen, the kidney, and acid and base. It also covers body fluids, energy use, growth z-scores, and plant water potential.
- **Neurons and excitable cells**: resting and ion potentials, and how a cell charges. Run the classic squid axon model, find a threshold, and test synapses, cables, and nerve speed.
- **Pharmacokinetics and drug response**: drug levels over time after one dose or many. It also finds the area under the curve, scales a dose, fits drug release, and models drug effect.
- **Binding, enzyme, and dose-response fits**: fit enzyme rates, inhibitors, and binding data. Read unknowns off a standard curve, and find an EC50 or a lethal dose.

## Lab and field

- **Bench calculations**: solutions and dilutions, buffers, centrifuge speed, cell counts, plate counts, MOI, growth rates, qPCR, microscopy, and calibration curves.
- **Biodiversity and field sampling**: diversity indices, richness estimates, rarefaction, similarity, quadrats and transects, and mark-recapture.

## Populations and models

- **Population dynamics**: growth models, harvesting, predator and prey, competition, metapopulations, island biogeography, and the logistic map.
- **Life tables and demography**: life tables, fertility rates, Leslie matrices, projections, and population pyramids.
- **Compartment, reaction, and agent models**: epidemic models (SIR and its relatives), reaction networks, and agent models on grids and networks. Reaction networks run as equations and as seeded random runs.

## Sources and limits

- Each result names its source when it uses published values, such as the Hodgkin-Huxley (1952) rates or the Severinghaus (1979) equation.
- The growth z-score takes L, M, and S from the public-domain CDC 2000 tables, which you look up for the age and sex.
- Fits report standard errors, and the linear plots for enzymes and binding are shown for comparison only.
