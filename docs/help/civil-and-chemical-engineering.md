# Civil, environmental, and chemical engineering tools

Nine tools share one window. Press Ctrl+K and type the tool's name, such as "Open hydrology and open channels", then pick a calculation from the list at the top. These tools are new in Beta, so check the answers against your own work while you learn them.

## How every tool works

- Type the inputs. A field with a unit has a unit list beside it, in SI and US units, and the answer follows the unit you pick.
- Tables take one row per line, with numbers split by spaces or commas.
- Under the answer are the results, the worked steps with their equations, any notes on assumptions, and a plot when the calculation has one.
- Insert answer puts the answer on the page. More ways to insert adds the worked steps, the result table, the equation in LaTeX, the data table, or a picture of the plot.
- When a method or data set comes from a public source, its name is shown under the result.

## Structures and soils

- **Indeterminate structures and influence lines.** Solve a beam over many supports, with the table of each step. Find how a load moving along a beam or a truss changes one force. Get rough forces in a building frame under wind. Make a spectrum from an earthquake record.
- **Soil properties, consolidation, and strength.** Work out how much water and air a soil holds. Sort it into its class. Find how fast water flows through it, how far it settles, and how strong it is.
- **Earth pressure, foundations, and slopes.** Find the push of soil on a wall and check the wall. Find how much load a footing or a pile can carry. Check whether a slope will slide.

## Water

- **Hydrology and open channels.** Turn rain into runoff and a flood peak. Route a flood through a river or a pond. Find how often a flood comes. Size a channel, a weir, or a pipe loop.
- **Groundwater and wells.** Find how water moves through the ground, how a well draws it down, and how far a spill spreads.
- **Water quality and treatment.** Track the oxygen in a river below an outfall. Work out water hardness and how to soften it. Size the steps of a treatment plant.

## Transportation

- **Traffic, highways, surveying, and site planning.** Find how much traffic a road carries and how long queues grow. Time a signal. Lay out curves on a highway. Split trips among places and modes. Close a survey loop. Balance cut and fill. Check a lot against zoning limits.

## Chemical processes

- **Material balances and separations.** Balance the flows through mixers, splitters, and recycle loops. Add the heat of a reaction. Find boiling points of a mixture. Count the stages a column needs.
- **Chemical and biological reactors.** Size a tank or tube reactor for a rate you type. Compare reactors in a row. Find the steady states of a hot reactor. Read a tracer test. Grow cells, and size an aeration tank.
