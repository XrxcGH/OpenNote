# My handwriting

OpenNote can write typed text and math in your own handwriting. It learns your hand from a calibration page you write with your pen, and keeps what it learns only on this device.

## Write the calibration page

Open Settings, then My handwriting, and choose Start calibration. The page goes one step at a time:

1. Small letters, capital letters, digits, then punctuation and symbols. Write each one in its own box, three times, the way you usually write.
2. A few sentences on the lines. They use every letter and the punctuation, so OpenNote learns your spacing, slant, and how you join letters.
3. A preview of your style, with what OpenNote measured: the size of your small letters, your slant, the space between letters and words, how often you join letters, and your pressure.

Each box saves as you write it. Choose Stop for now to come back later; Resume calibration picks up where you stopped. To write a box again, choose Redo on it, or press Delete while it has focus. The arrow keys move between boxes, and Home and End go to the first and last.

The calibration page is the only way a style begins. OpenNote never asks for or keeps a signature.

## Write in your handwriting

- **Write in my handwriting**: select typed text, then choose Write in my handwriting from the text menu or the command palette (Ctrl+K). Choose whether the handwriting replaces the text or goes beside it. Math in the text, typed or from the equation editor, comes out as handwritten math.
- **Type as handwriting**: turn it on in the Draw tab, then type. Each word appears in your hand as you type and becomes ink when it ends, so Undo takes back one word at a time. Backspace unwrites letter by letter. Click the page to move where the writing goes, and press Escape to stop.

The writing is ordinary ink in the current pen's color and width. You can erase it, move it, and recolor it like any other ink. On ruled, grid, or dot paper it sits on the paper's lines, and it wraps at the width of the text it replaces. Each letter takes one of the ways you wrote it, with small changes of size and angle within your own range.

If you never wrote a character, OpenNote uses a clean neutral hand for it and tells you which ones. Choose Add them to write them in the Math and science section.

## Math and science

Choose Write math and science symbols in Settings, My handwriting, to write Greek letters, operators such as the integral and sum signs, arrows, accents for vectors and hats, and brackets, small and tall. Typed math then lays out in your hand: fractions with their bar, raised and lowered scripts, roots, integrals and sums with their limits, matrices, and lines lined up at their equals signs.

## Add from your own writing

Once the calibration page is done, you can add scans, photos, or PDFs of your own handwriting, such as worked problems, formula sheets, chemistry, labeled diagrams, or tables. In Settings, My handwriting, select the Choose files… button.

- PDFs exported from OneNote and other note apps keep handwriting as drawing, which OpenNote reads directly. Scanned pages and photos are traced from the picture.
- OpenNote finds the symbols and shows each one with what it reads it as. Correct any that are wrong, skip any that are not yours or not one symbol, then choose Add.
- OpenNote also learns your math layout from them: how small and how high you write exponents, how far a fraction bar reaches, and how tall you draw integral signs.
- Uploads only ever add to your style. The files are dropped once they are read, unless you turn on Keep the files with my style.

Uploads are read as untrusted files: very large files are refused, and a file that can't be read is reported without changing your style.

## Privacy

Your handwriting style stays on this device, encrypted. Its key is kept in Windows Credential Manager. The style is not synced, exported, searched, or put in logs or crash reports.

To remove it, choose Delete my handwriting. This deletes the calibration page, the style, any kept uploads, and the key. Ink you already wrote stays on your pages.
